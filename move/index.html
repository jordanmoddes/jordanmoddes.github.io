01 What You Actually Net After Tax

Since the TCJA moving expense deduction was made permanent by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025), your lump sum is fully taxable ordinary income. No deduction offsets it. Federal supplemental withholding is 22%, but if your total income puts you in the 24% bracket, you'll owe the difference at filing. Pennsylvania also has a 3.07% flat income tax (and NJ has 5.53% at this income level) — factor in whichever state applies to your residency when you receive the payment.

04 Trade-offs to Close the Gap at $5K
  • 🛋️
    Sell heavy furniture before you leave. A couch, bed frame, dresser, and dining set could realistically bring $400–900 on Facebook Marketplace (Austin market is active). Smaller truck size = lower DIY rental cost. Reduces the load enough to potentially drop one truck size, saving $200–400 in rental fees and reducing gas by ~$50–80.
  • 🚚
    Container over truck if you can wait on delivery. PODS/Pack-Rat on a flexible delivery window can come in at $1,800–2,000 — comparable to DIY truck rental but without the driving burden. You'd still need to get yourself to PA separately (one-way flight ~$150–250).
  • 🚗
    Drive the car, don't ship it. Saves $1,100–1,400 vs. open transport, costs $200–300 in gas and 2 days of time. Non-negotiable at $5K — car shipping is the first thing to cut.
  • 🏠
    Land on the Phillipsburg NJ side. Median rent ~$1,595 vs. Easton PA ~$1,700–1,730. Small difference per month but also means a smaller deposit. NJ also still allows a state-level moving expense deduction — worth checking at tax time.
  • 📅
    Align your new lease start date with move-out. Avoid paying any temporary lodging. Even 5 nights at an Extended Stay adds $375–500 you don't have room for at $5K.
  • 💳
    Use a 0% intro APR card strategically. If you have a card with a 0% window (many offer 12–15 months), float the deposit and first month's rent on credit and pay it off from your first few paychecks. This is NOT a long-term solution but it removes the cash-on-hand crunch at move time.
  • ⚠️
    The real risk at $5K un-grossed: You genuinely cannot do this move out-of-pocket at $3,363 net unless you arrive with ~$5,500+ in personal savings to bridge the gap. The lease termination alone consumes more than the entire net payment. This isn't a tight budget situation — it's a structural shortfall that requires either negotiating the package, negotiating the lease fee, or arriving with cash.
05 What to Ask Your Employer — Before You Commit
Questions for HR / Comp Team
  1. Is the lump sum grossed up? Specifically: will you receive $10,000 net in hand, or $10,000 gross before withholding? This is the single most important question — it changes your actual budget by $3,270.
  2. When is the lump sum paid? Before your start date, with your first paycheck, or on a reimbursement schedule? Timing affects your cash flow at the moment of the move and determines your state tax exposure (TX resident = no state tax).
  3. Is there a clawback provision? Most relocation agreements require repayment on a pro-rated schedule if you leave within 1–2 years. Know this before you sign.
  4. Can you negotiate the amount? The industry benchmark for a renter relocation is ~$14,600–21,800. A $10K gross offer (especially un-grossed-up) is on the low end. If this is a Senior Ops Manager offer at $130K+, pushing for $15K or a grossed-up $12K is reasonable.
  5. Does the offer cover direct expenses vs. lump sum? If your employer will pay the lease termination fee directly (rather than reimbursing it through your lump sum), that $4,290 is off the table and your entire lump sum is available for moving costs.
